How to Build a Skincare Tech Routine (Without Wasting Money)

A practical order of operations for LED masks, microcurrent devices, and steamers.

Skincare tech is easy to buy and surprisingly easy to use wrong. Most of the disappointment people report with LED masks, microcurrent tools, and facial steamers has nothing to do with the device itself — it comes down to the order things happen in, how long someone actually stuck with it, and whether they were judging results after three sessions instead of three weeks. Here's the routine logic that makes these devices worth the money.

Start with one device, not three

LED masks, microcurrent tools, and RF devices all ask for consistent use over weeks before they show anything. Buying all three at once and rotating between them usually means none of them get used long enough to matter — you end up with three half-habits instead of one real one. Pick the device that addresses your biggest concern first (see our LED mask guide and microcurrent & RF guide for the differences), commit to it for a month, and only then decide whether a second device earns a place in the routine.

Clean skin first, every time

Serums, sunscreen, and makeup residue sit on top of your skin and block light and current from actually reaching it. A device used on unwashed or half-cleansed skin is mostly wasted effort, no matter how good the device is. This is the single most common reason someone says a mask or microcurrent tool "doesn't do anything" — the problem usually isn't the device.

Know where your device goes in the routine

As a general order: cleanse, use your device (LED mask, microcurrent, or steamer), then apply serums and moisturizer. Devices work on bare or just-steamed skin, and your serums absorb better into skin that's already been treated rather than fighting through a layer of product first. If you use a facial steamer, that comes before everything else — it opens pores and preps skin for whatever comes next, whether that's a device or just your regular serum.

Track weeks, not days

Nearly every skincare device is cumulative. A phone photo taken under the same lighting, once a week, is a far more honest progress check than judging results after a handful of sessions. Most people who give up on a device do it in week one or two — right before the point where consistent users typically start to notice a difference.

When it's time to add a second device

Once your first device is a genuine habit — meaning you reach for it without having to remind yourself — adding a second one is reasonable. Pair devices that do different jobs rather than two that overlap: an LED mask for light therapy plus a microcurrent tool for muscle stimulation covers more ground than two tools doing roughly the same thing.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use an LED mask and a microcurrent device on the same day?

Yes, most people use them back to back — LED mask first, then microcurrent, since light therapy doesn't interfere with the electrical current. Leave a few minutes between the two and always apply a conductive gel before microcurrent use.

Should I use my skincare device before or after moisturizer?

Before, in almost all cases. LED masks, microcurrent tools, and steamers work best on clean or just-steamed skin — apply your serums and moisturizer afterward so they absorb into skin the device has already treated.

Why am I not seeing results yet?

Check three things first: are you using the device on clean skin, are you using it consistently (most devices need 4-8 weeks), and are you comparing photos taken in the same lighting. Most "it's not working" cases turn out to be one of these three.
